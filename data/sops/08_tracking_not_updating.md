# Tracking Not Updating

## Purpose
This SOP outlines the standard process for handling customer inquiries regarding shipments that show no movement or appear "stuck" on the tracking page. The goal is to provide accurate status updates and manage customer expectations regarding delivery timelines.

## When this applies
Use this procedure when a customer contacts support via the web form reporting that their tracking information has not updated for 24 hours or longer, or if they are concerned that their parcel is not progressing toward the destination.

## Steps for the agent
1. **Verify the order status:** Check the internal order management system to confirm the actual dispatch date.
2. **Calculate delivery status:**
   - Standard shipping: 3–5 business days.
   - Express shipping: 1–2 business days.
   - A parcel is officially "late" only once it is 2 business days past the estimated delivery date.
   - A parcel is considered "lost" only after 7 business days past the estimated delivery date.
3. **Check carrier updates:** Review the carrier’s portal for transit alerts or local delays. Occasionally, tracking updates may lag due to high volumes at regional sorting hubs.
4. **Communication:** If the parcel is within the standard delivery window, reassure the customer that updates often resume once the package reaches the next regional hub.
5. **Lost Parcels:** If the parcel meets the criteria for "lost" (7 business days past the estimated date), initiate a formal trace with the carrier and notify the customer that a replacement or refund will be processed if the item cannot be located within 48 hours.

## What to tell the customer
"Thank you for reaching out; I have checked your order status and can confirm your parcel is currently in transit with our carrier. Tracking updates can sometimes be delayed while a package moves between regional distribution centers, but your order is still within our expected delivery window. If your tracking does not update within the next 48 hours, please let us know so we can investigate further."

## Escalation and priority
*   **Low:** The tracking is stagnant but the estimated delivery date is still 3 or more business days away.
*   **Medium:** The parcel is officially "late" (2 business days past the estimated delivery date).
*   **High:** The parcel is confirmed "lost" (7 business days past the estimated delivery date).
*   **Urgent:** If multiple customers report the same carrier or region is experiencing a total delivery stoppage, escalate to the Logistics manager immediately. 

## Common mistakes
*   **Promising specific delivery times:** Never guarantee a delivery date if the carrier has not provided one, as this leads to future dissatisfaction.
*   **Ignoring the "lost" threshold:** Do not offer refunds or replacements before the 7-day "lost" window has passed, unless there is clear evidence of a terminal transit error.
*   **Miscalculating business days:** Always exclude Sundays and public holidays when calculating the "late" or "lost" status.
